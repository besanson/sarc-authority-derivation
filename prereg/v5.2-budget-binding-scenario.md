## What this closes

The Fourth amendment (`NOVELTY.md`, v0.4, `prereg/v3.1-isolated-arms.md`)
found, by direct diagnostic over the full 349,020-decision sweep, that
`spend_against_depleted_delegated_budget` (`losses.py`: `t.order_value >
t.budget_remaining`) fires zero times across all 30 registered seeds x
2 workflows, under the declared `PERIOD_BUDGET_MULTIPLIER = 1.15`
(`experiments.py`). The paper draft's own Threats to validity (Section
9) states this plainly and leaves it open: "A different declared loss
model, budget multiplier, or seed set could still see it bind." This
document registers, and the next commit runs, exactly that -- not a
different loss model or seed set, but a deliberately tighter budget
multiplier, so the mechanism this project's own code already implements
is actually exercised by the sweep at least once, and CH-A1's
conclusion is checked under a scenario where it could, in principle,
have come out differently.

## The scenario (registered, hand-reasoned, not tuned after seeing a result)

**One new parameter value**: `period_budget_multiplier = 0.25` (a
quarter of the median per-period demand), applied through the SAME
`calibrate_period_budget`/`run_seed_workflow` machinery
(`experiments.py`), over the SAME 30 registered seeds
(`prereg/seeds.json`) and both workflows -- no new seeds, no new loss
model, exactly the one parameter this document names. `1.15` was found
empirically to bind zero times despite its own comment's stated intent
("deliberately tight enough to bind sometimes") -- `0.25` is chosen
deliberately far below that, not as a second guess at "realistic," but
as a stress value: since `calibrate_period_budget`'s own budget is
reset to this value at the start of EVERY period and only decreases as
decisions are admitted within it, a quarter of the MEDIAN period's
total demand should be exceeded partway through most periods (not just
the above-median half), making it exceedingly likely that at least one
decision, in at least one period, in at least one seed x workflow cell,
arrives after the remaining budget has already fallen below that
decision's own `order_value` -- for reasons already reachable from
`calibrate_period_budget`'s own docstring ("binds on above-median
periods without being either always-empty or never-binding" was `1.15`'s
own stated design intent for a MODEST bind rate; `0.25` is registered
here specifically to push well past modest into "should bind on most
periods," a deliberately different, cruder operating point, not a
refinement of the same one).

**Registered as a genuinely two-sided check, not assumed**: it remains
possible, though thought unlikely given the reasoning above, that even
`0.25` does not bind on this specific real dataset's actual order-value
distribution -- if so, that is itself reported honestly (a real,
explainable finding about the data, the same standing this project
gives every registered negative outcome), not silently replaced with a
tighter value chosen after the fact to force a bind.

## Decision rule (two-sided)

Run the full 30 x 2 sweep under `period_budget_multiplier = 0.25`,
using the existing `run_seed_workflow`/aggregation machinery
(`experiments.py`/`sweep.py`'s own `mean_ci95`, unmodified), writing a
SEPARATE result (this scenario's own output path, not overwriting the
frozen v0.4 `out/results/sweep_summary.json`, which used `1.15` and
stays exactly as committed). Report:

1. **Does `spend_against_depleted_delegated_budget` fire at least once**
   across the new sweep (`derived`'s own admission decisions, the same
   "true" trajectory `prereg/v3.1-isolated-arms.md`'s own decision-rule
   table already designates) -- the positive-control question this
   scenario exists to answer. Reported as a plain count, per cell and
   in aggregate.
2. **CH-A1 under this scenario**: `derived_missed` (hypothesized zero,
   per CH-A1's own decision rule, `prereg/hypotheses.md` -- derived's
   own admission rule reads the SAME loss registry the ground truth is
   defined by, a checked structural consequence, not independent of the
   scenario) and `baseline_missed` (the empirical quantity), reported
   the same way `sweep.py`'s existing `aggregate_sweep` already reports
   them for the frozen v0.4 sweep, so the two are directly comparable
   side by side.
3. **Whether CH-A1's conclusion (not necessarily its magnitude) holds**:
   SUPPORTED under this scenario iff `derived_missed` is zero on every
   cell where the budget loss actually fired at least once (the cells
   where this scenario's own mechanism was actually exercised) --
   registered here, before running, as the specific, checkable claim
   "derived-P*'s structural zero-miss guarantee holds even on the
   decisions this new scenario's own tighter budget newly causes to be
   denied," not a restatement of the original CH-A1 result under a
   parameter that never exercised the mechanism it is being asked
   about.

Both outcomes -- the budget genuinely binding or not, and CH-A1 holding
or not under this scenario -- are valid, reportable results. Nothing
about the frozen v0.4 sweep, `prereg/hypotheses.md`'s own CH-A1 text, or
`PERIOD_BUDGET_MULTIPLIER`'s existing declared value of `1.15` is
edited or retuned by this document or what it registers.

  behaviour. This is plumbing to reach the registered scenario above,
  not a new experiment mechanism of its own.
