### The new domain: a data-and-communications agent

An agent (a support bot, an analytics pipeline, a marketing-automation
job, an admin tool) processes or transmits one record. Eight candidate
observations, each a per-decision field with a small declared finite
domain (exhaustive sweep, matching v1's/v2's/v4's own convention -- no
continuous or unbounded domain):

| # | Property | Declared domain |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `data_category` | `{public, internal, personal, special_category}` |
| 2 | `legal_basis` | `{consent, contract, legitimate_interest, legal_obligation, none_declared}` |
| 3 | `recipient_type` | `{internal_only, processor_under_contract, third_party_controller}` |
| 4 | `destination_region` | `{eea, adequacy_country, third_country_with_safeguards, third_country_no_safeguards}` |
| 5 | `retention_status` | `{within_declared_period, beyond_declared_period}` |
| 6 | `channel_encryption` | `{encrypted, unencrypted}` |
| 7 | `erasure_request_state` | `{none, honored, pending_unhandled}` |
| 8 | `actor_role` | `{agent-support-bot, agent-analytics-pipeline, agent-marketing-automation, agent-admin}` |

Rank-0 (fully unconstrained) size: 4x5x3x4x2x2x3x4 = 11,520 tuples.

**Declared reachability rule** (mirroring v4's own "production branch
implies production environment" pairing -- one meaningful cross-field
constraint, not a pure unconstrained product): `recipient_type ==
internal_only` implies `destination_region == eea` (data that never
leaves the organisation cannot, by construction, be a cross-border
transfer). This narrows the `recipient_type` x `destination_region`
product from 12 pairs to 9 (internal_only pairs only with eea; the other
two recipient types pair with all four regions), giving a reachable set
of 4x5x9x2x2x3x4 = **8,640 tuples**.

**Loss predicates** (six, transcribed from the GDPR and the ePrivacy
Directive -- both fetch-verified below, not asserted from memory, the
same discipline `prereg/v4-realistic-domain.md`'s own citations
followed):

1. **`unlawful_processing`**. `legal_basis == none_declared`. Source:
   GDPR Art. 6(1) (Regulation (EU) 2016/679) -- processing is lawful
   only if at least one of the enumerated bases applies; declaring none
   is itself the violation this predicate names.
2. **`special_category_without_basis`**. `data_category ==
   special_category` AND `legal_basis in {none_declared,
   legitimate_interest}`. Source: GDPR Art. 9(1)/9(2) -- Art. 9(2)
   enumerates ten specific exceptions (explicit consent, employment law,
   vital interests, and others); legitimate interest (the Art. 6(1)(f)
   basis) is confirmed NOT among them (fetch-verified directly,
   2026-09-08: gdpr-info.eu's own Art. 9 rendering lists the ten Art.
   9(2) exceptions and states plainly legitimate interest is not one),
   so a `legitimate_interest` basis that would suffice for ordinary
   data under Art. 6 does not suffice for special-category data under
   Art. 9 specifically.
3. **`unsafeguarded_transfer`**. `destination_region ==
   third_country_no_safeguards` AND `data_category != public`. Source:
   GDPR Art. 44 (Chapter V's general principle for transfers) -- a
   transfer "shall take place only if the conditions laid down in this
   Chapter are complied with," which `third_country_no_safeguards`
   names as failing by construction.
4. **`retention_violation`**. `retention_status ==
   beyond_declared_period` AND `data_category != public`. Source: GDPR
   Art. 5(1)(e), the storage-limitation principle.
5. **`unconfidential_channel`**. `channel_encryption == unencrypted`
   AND `data_category in {personal, special_category}`. Source:
   Directive 2002/58/EC (the ePrivacy Directive) Art. 5(1) --
   confidentiality of communications, prohibiting interception or
   storage of communications content without consent.
6. **`unfulfilled_erasure`**. `erasure_request_state ==
   pending_unhandled` AND `data_category != public`. Source: GDPR Art.
   17, the right to erasure.

`actor_role` is deliberately read by none of the six predicates above --
an unsurprising, structurally-forced non-participant (the same kind
v4's own `actor_identity`/`deployment_window` already are), named here
in advance, not discovered as a surprise later.

**No core/reduct outcome is asserted in advance.** Unlike the toy
fixtures elsewhere in this project, this domain's 8,640-tuple reachable
set is not hand-enumerable; v4's own 51,840-tuple domain was
machine-checked, not hand-enumerated, and this domain is registered the
same way -- structurally reasoned about (the reachability rule, the
non-participation of `actor_role`), not pre-computed. The core-versus-
reduct outcome is measured in Step 4, two-sided, exactly as CH-B1 was.

## Per-domain YAML packaging (all three domains)

Each of the three domains ships five files under
`authority_bench_domains/<domain>/`:

- **`candidate-context.yaml`**: `candidate_properties`, each with its
  declared domain (the table above, or the equivalent already-declared
  Python structure for v2/v4).
- **`loss-model.yaml`**: one entry per loss predicate, naming its
  citation id (`verified-citations.json`).
- **`reachability.yaml`**: the domain's own declared reachability
  rule(s) (v2: the `min_order_quantity` cross-field filter; v4: the two
  rules from `prereg/v4-realistic-domain.md`; data-and-communications:
  the rule above).
- **`baseline-manual-policy.yaml`**: the hand-authored manual
  least-privilege baseline (Step 3) for that domain, with its own
  stated rationale.
- **`expected-certificates.yaml`**: for v2/v4, the ALREADY-COMMITTED,
  already machine-checked core/reduct facts (CH-A8/CH-A10, CH-B1),
  restated for AuthorityBench's own consumption, not re-derived; for
  data-and-communications, only the structural facts this document
  itself defends above (the reachability rule's effect on reachable-set
  size; `actor_role`'s forced non-participation) -- not a pre-computed
  core or reduct.

These YAML files are a PACKAGING of what `domain.py`/`domain_v4.py`/the
new domain module already declare (or will declare) in Python, read by
`authority_bench.py`'s own harness for domain-agnostic dispatch -- they
do not replace or retune the existing Python declarations (v2's and
v4's own modules are unmodified; "science of committed versions stays
frozen").

## The four baselines

1. **Manual least-privilege** (hand-authored per domain, registered
   here, not derived by any formula -- a good-faith guess at what a
   competent, time-pressured reviewer would pick by inspection, not a
   strawman):
   - **v2**: `{actor_role, order_value, resource_class,
     budget_remaining}` -- the "obviously relevant" business-risk
     properties; a manual reviewer is registered here as plausibly
     missing `grant_id`/`consumed_grant_ids` (replay-prevention
     machinery, not obvious without deliberate replay-threat
     modelling), `day`/`frozen` (temporal/freeze-state conditions), and
     `min_order_quantity`.
   - **v4**: `{delegated_role, operation, approval_token,
     environment}` -- a conventional RBAC-plus-change-control set; a
     manual reviewer is registered here as plausibly missing
     `resource_owner` (cross-tenant isolation, easy to overlook without
     deliberate multi-tenant threat modelling -- CH-B1's own
     counterexample pair already turns on exactly this kind of gap),
     `repository`, and `data_classification`.
   - **data-and-communications**: `{data_category, legal_basis,
     destination_region, channel_encryption}` -- the "obviously
     GDPR-flavoured" properties; a manual reviewer is registered here
     as plausibly missing `retention_status` and
     `erasure_request_state` (ongoing-obligation controls, easy to
     treat as a process concern rather than a gating one).

   Each is measured for `correctness` (Definition 4 sufficiency,
   checked directly, with a concrete counterexample pair reported if
   not) exactly like every other baseline -- the hypothesis that
   manual least-privilege under-covers is registered, two-sided, not
   assumed: a manual policy turning out sufficient on some domain is a
   valid, reportable outcome, not a contradiction.

2. **ABAC policy mining, reimplemented from Xu and Stoller**
   (`mining_baseline.py`, already committed and unmodified by this
   document -- `prereg/v6-authority-bench.md`'s own scoped
   seed-and-generalize algorithm). **Gated on validation** (registered
   here, checked at Step 3 runtime): before this baseline is used in
   Step 4's comparison, its own output is checked against at least one
   independently-verifiable, published quantity from Xu and Stoller's
   own paper (`xu-stoller-abac-mining`) or its freely-available arXiv
   preprint (arXiv:1306.2401) -- specifically, whether this scoped
   reimplementation, run on a small worked example constructed to match
   a case their paper describes (or, if no such reproducible case is
   locatable within the paper's own published text, the closest
   available published summary statistic, e.g. a reported rule count or
   reduction ratio on one of their named case studies), lands within a
   registered, generous tolerance of their reported number. **This is a
   real gate, not a formality**: if no such comparison is achievable
   (their published paper does not include a from-scratch-reproducible
   dataset, only aggregate results over private/proprietary policy
   corpora this project has no access to -- a real possibility,
   assessed honestly at Step 3, not assumed away here) OR the
   comparison is achievable but fails to land within tolerance, this
   baseline is reported **excluded: unvalidated** in Step 4's results
   and is not used in the benchmark tables -- exactly the contingency
   the author registered. `mining_baseline.py`'s own already-passing
   unit tests (the majority-of-three hand derivation) are necessary but
   not sufficient for this gate: they check the algorithm's OWN
   internal correctness against this project's own worked example, not
   agreement with Xu and Stoller's own published numbers.

3. **Essential-variable analysis.** A classical, independently-sourced
   formal method from switching/Boolean-function theory: a variable
   (property) is *essential* for a function iff there exist inputs
   differing only in that variable with different outputs -- Salomaa,
   A., "On essential variables of functions, especially in the algebra
   of logic," Ann. Acad. Sci. Fenn. Ser. A I. Math. 339 (1963), 3-11
   (the foundational citation, fetch-verified 2026-09-08 via its
   citation in Couceiro, M. and Lehtonen, E., "On the effect of
   variable identification on the essential arity of functions," freely
   available at arXiv:math/0701332, which states the same definition
   explicitly: "there are elements $a_1,\ldots,a_n,b \in A$ such that
   $f(a_1,\ldots,a_i,\ldots,a_n) \neq f(a_1,\ldots,a_{i-1},b,a_{i+1},
   \ldots,a_n)$"). **This definition is, term for term, Definition 1**
   (`participation.py`'s own docstring: "property p is authority-bearing
   for loss model M iff there exist executable-reachable tuples a, a'
   differing only in p whose M-verdicts differ") -- an independent
   discovery of the identical mathematical criterion from a different
   formal tradition (switching theory, 1963) than the one this
   project's own core already cites (rough sets, Pawlak 1982; Skowron
   and Rauszer 1992). Registered here as its own named baseline for
   exactly that reason: it is expected, not merely permitted, to return
   the IDENTICAL set `reduct.compute_core` already computes (both
   `AuthorityBench`'s own harness call and `reduct.compute_core` share
   the same underlying witness search, `participation.compute_p_star`,
   unmodified) -- a genuine two-sided check is still run (Step 4
   confirms the identity directly, not by definition alone), and on a
   domain where the core is insufficient (v4, and plausibly the new
   domain), this baseline inherits that insufficiency, honestly, not
   smoothed over.

4. **Exhaustive reduct.** `reduct.exact_reducts()`, unmodified,
   already committed -- reported here as its OWN named baseline row
   (not merely the implicit standard `src/authority_compiler`'s
   `minimum_cardinality_reduct` is checked against), since v4's own two
   equal-cardinality reducts mean "the exhaustive answer" is genuinely
   a SET of valid contracts, not a single one, worth reporting as such
   rather than collapsed into one row.

## Metrics (six, per baseline per domain)

- **Correctness**: Definition 4 sufficiency, checked directly
  (`reduct.sufficiency`) -- boolean, with a counterexample pair
  attached when false.
- **Contract size**: cardinality of the returned attribute set.
- **Contract cost**: the declared observation-cost model
  `prereg/v5-synthesis.md` already registers (`latency + privacy +
  staleness + failure_probability`, `benchmarks.py`'s own
  `declared_cost`, floor 0.01) -- applied here with REAL declared
  per-property costs for the new data-and-communications domain
  (registered in `candidate-context.yaml`, Step 2: fields already
  present in the request/decision context at zero round-trip cost,
  e.g. `data_category`, `legal_basis`, score low on `latency`;
  `destination_region`/`retention_status`, which this domain's own
  design implies may require a lookup against a separate record store,
  score higher), not the uniform floor `benchmarks.py`'s own
  `_real_domain_costs` used for v1/v2/v4 when no real cost data was
  declared for them -- v2/v4 keep that uniform floor here too (no new
  cost data is retroactively invented for domains whose own preregs
  never declared any), reported honestly as a metric that is only
  informative for the new domain.
- **Runtime**: wall-clock seconds (`time.perf_counter()`), per
  baseline per domain.
- **Memory**: peak RSS delta (`resource.getrusage(resource.RUSAGE_SELF)
  .ru_maxrss`, `benchmarks.py`'s own established pattern), per baseline
  per domain.
- **Counterexamples**: reported wherever correctness is false (a
  concrete reachable pair agreeing on the baseline's attributes with
  differing verdicts) -- `reduct.sufficiency`'s own counterexample
  output, not a separate search.

## `src/authority_compiler`'s fuller API (Step 1, registered here)

`derive_authority_contract(loss_model, reachable_semantics,
candidate_context, observation_costs)` -- four parameters, replacing
the initial `(candidate_properties, reachable, registry)` signature
(`prereg/v6-authority-bench.md`'s own E1): `candidate_context` names the
candidate properties and their declared domains (the YAML structure
above); `reachable_semantics` is the callable/iterable producing the
executable-reachable set; `loss_model` is the declared registry;
`observation_costs`, when supplied, enables the cost-based fields below
(when omitted, those fields report `None`, not a fabricated cost).
Returns a dataclass with nine fields: `core_attributes` (Definition 2),
`minimal_reducts` (Definition 5, exhaustive -- `reduct.exact_reducts()`,
when tractable; falls back to reporting only
`minimum_cardinality_reduct` with `minimal_reducts: None` and a stated
reason when exhaustion is not affordable, the same "where exhaustion is
feasible" scoping `synthesis.py`'s own module docstring already uses),
`minimum_cardinality_reduct` (`synthesis.py`'s SAT/MaxSAT backend),
`minimum_cost_reduct` (same backend, cost-weighted, `None` when no
`observation_costs` supplied), `redundant_attributes` (Definition 3,
the coverage list), `sufficiency_certificate` (the FULL certificate
dict `reduct.sufficiency` returns for the core, not merely a boolean),
`counterexamples` (the certificate's own counterexample when the core
is not sufficient), `reachability_dependencies` (the domain's own
declared reachability rules, read from `candidate_context`/
`reachable_semantics`, not derived -- named here as a pass-through
field, since Definition 1's own participation criterion is already
computed against whatever `reachable_semantics` actually produces, and
callers reading `reachability_dependencies` can see WHY a property did
or did not participate without re-deriving it), and
`contract_change_delta` (a bound method, not a precomputed value --
`synthesis.contract_change_delta` wired through with this call's own
`candidate_context`/`loss_model` closed over, so a caller supplies only
`new_tuples` later).

The paper pipeline (`populate_paper.py`/`paper_tables.py`) becomes a
consumer of this package for every already-existing v1/v2/v4-derived
paper slot it currently computes by calling `participation.py`/
`reduct.py`/`synthesis.py` directly -- re-plumbing, not re-deriving: the
same underlying calls, through the packaged entry point, with the paper
draft's own already-committed numbers required to stay byte-identical
(release-check's own populated-draft-freshness gate already enforces
this).

`pyproject.toml` gains a `[project.scripts]` entry, `authority-bench =
"authority_bench:main"` -- an installable CLI command, registered here
before Step 1 adds it, not assumed to already exist (this repo currently
has no `[project]`/packaging section at all, `pyproject.toml`'s own
header comment: "This repo has no packaging config of its own (it is
run in place, not installed)" -- Step 1 adds the minimal `[project]`
table this entry point requires, without changing how the rest of the
repo is run, i.e. `python3 <script>.py` keeps working unchanged for
every other entry point).
