## Motivation and disposition of v5.1

`prereg/v5.1-discernibility-scaling.md` scaled the *reachable-set size*
(up to 10^5 tuples) while deliberately keeping the *candidate-property
count* small (5 to 7) specifically so exhaustive cross-check
(`reduct.exact_reducts()`) stayed feasible at every registered size, and
kept the discernibility family, after superset removal, at exactly one
set -- a deliberately minimal structure chosen to isolate the
tuple-count axis cleanly. It answered the question it was built for
(does the discernibility-family construction and each synthesis backend
scale as the reachable set grows) and does not, and was never
registered to, answer a different one: what happens when the
*candidate-property count* itself is large enough that exhaustive
verification stops being available at all, and the discernibility
structure is rich rather than minimal.

This document registers that different benchmark. v5.1's own result
stays frozen, cited, and is not retroactively edited or relabelled
`exploratory` -- unlike the six-tuple benchmark's disposition relative
to v5.1 itself (`NOVELTY.md`'s Ninth amendment), v5.1 answered a
genuine, still-relevant question about a different axis (reachable-set
growth at small, exhaustible `n`) that this document does not
supersede. Once this benchmark's real results are committed, v5.1 is
referred to in every document below as **"the tuple-scaling result"**
-- kept, cited, named, not exploratory -- while this document's own
result becomes *the* primary scaling table (the combinatorial-hardness
axis: growing `n` far past exhaustibility, with a rich discernibility
structure and a genuine SAT-vs-MaxSAT comparison v5.1's own
two-reduct/one-set family was too simple to exercise).

## The combinatorial-hardness family (registered construction, hand-derived and proved below)

Three registered families, one planted minimum-reduct size paired with
one candidate-attribute-universe size each (not a 3x3 cross product):

| Family | Signal size `k` (planted minimum reduct) | Candidate attributes `n` | Diversity flags `D` | Padding depth `J` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | 5  | 30  | 25 | 8 |
| B | 10 | 60  | 25 | 8 |
| C | 15 | 100 | 25 | 8 |

**Candidate properties** (all boolean), per family:
- **Signal** `S = {p_1, ..., p_k}`.
- **Diversity flags** `F = {f_1, ..., f_25}` (`D = 25` in every family --
  the common constant that makes a single shared construction and a
  single shared padding depth `J = 8` work across all three sizes, see
  below).
- **Padding** `Z`, size `n - k - 25` (0 for Family A, 25 for Family B,
  60 for Family C) -- held at `0` on *every* reachable tuple, never
  varied. Pure bookkeeping to reach the registered `n`; proved below to
  never appear in any difference set, by never differing between any
  two reachable tuples.

**Verdict** (`benchmarks.py`'s own established style of naming a
synthetic predicate without a "loss" reading, `run_scaling_experiment`'s
`"all_signal_properties_set"` the direct precedent, generalized from a
fixed `k=5` to the three registered sizes): `all_signal_set(t) = `
`(t.p_1 == 1) and ... and (t.p_k == 1)`. Depends on `S` alone --
flags and padding never appear in its definition.

**Reachable set** (exactly `1 + 200*k` tuples, by construction, not
sampled -- `200 = D * J = 25 * 8`, the same product in every family):
- One tuple `T_0`: every signal property `1`, every flag `0`, every
  padding property `0`.
- For every signal position `i` (`1..k`), every flag offset `e`
  (`1..25`), every padding depth `j` (`1..8`): one tuple `T[i,e,j]`:
  signal `= S` with position `i` set to `0` (every other signal
  property `1`), flags `= ON` at positions `{((e - 1 + t) mod 25) + 1 :
  t = 0, ..., j - 1}` (a run of `j` flags, cyclically starting at `e`),
  every other flag and every padding property `0`.

This gives `1 + 5*200 = 1001` (Family A), `1 + 10*200 = 2001` (Family
B), and `1 + 15*200 = 3001` (Family C) reachable tuples -- all inside
the registered `10^3` to `10^4` range, via one shared formula
(`1 + 200*k`), not tuned per family after the fact.

## Hand-derivation, checked here before any code exists (registration discipline: not adjusted afterward to match a measured result)

All claims below were checked computationally against a toy-scale
instantiation of this exact construction (`k=2,D=4,J=3` and
`k=3,D=5,J=2`) run through this repository's own real, unmodified
`discernibility.py`/`reduct.py` as a pre-registration sanity check (not
part of the registered pipeline or its results, and not itself a
substitute for the proofs below, which hold for the actual registered
sizes) -- every prediction below matched exactly at toy scale, with zero
discrepancies, before this document was written.

- **`T_0`'s verdict is `1`; every `T[i,e,j]`'s verdict is `0`**:
  immediate from the verdict's own definition and each tuple's
  construction (`T_0` has all of `S` set; `T[i,e,j]` has `p_i = 0`).
- **All `1 + 200*k` reachable tuples are pairwise distinct**: `T_0` is
  the unique tuple with `S` entirely `1` (every `T[i,e,j]` has `p_i =
  0`). Among the `T[i,e,j]`, the signal pattern alone determines `i`
  (which single signal position is `0`), and for fixed `i`, the flag-run
  `{((e-1+t) mod 25)+1 : t=0..j-1}` -- a set of `j` cyclically
  consecutive positions among 25 -- has a unique `(e, j)` (starting
  position and length, for `j <= 8 < 25`), so distinct `(i, e, j)`
  triples give distinct tuples.
- **`S` is sufficient (Definition 4)**: the verdict is defined as a
  function of `S` alone, so two tuples agreeing on every property in
  `S` trivially share a verdict -- true by construction, not by
  checking the specific reachable set.
- **`S` is minimal**: take any proper subset `T subset-of S` and any
  `p_i in S \ T`. `T_0` and `T[i,e,j]` (any `e, j`) agree on `T` --
  both are `1` on every signal property except possibly `p_i`, which
  `T` excludes -- yet `T_0`'s verdict is `1` and `T[i,e,j]`'s is `0`.
  So every proper subset of `S` is insufficient, witnessed directly:
  **`S` (size `k`) is a reduct.**
- **`F` (all 25 flags) is sufficient**: `T_0` is the unique reachable
  tuple with every flag `0` (every `T[i,e,j]` has a nonempty flag-run
  set). So the partition cell for "`F`-projection `=` all-`0`" is the
  singleton `{T_0}` (verdict `1`, trivially uniform); every other cell
  contains only `T[i,e,j]` tuples (verdict `0` uniformly, regardless of
  how they group by `F`-projection). Both cases uniform: sufficient.
- **`F` is minimal**: remove any single flag `f` from `F`. `T[i,f,1]`
  (any signal position `i`, the one-hot run at exactly `f`) has every
  flag `0` *except* `f` -- so on `F \ {f}`, it shows all-`0`, matching
  `T_0`'s projection there, yet the two tuples' verdicts differ (`0`
  vs. `1`). So `F \ {f}` is insufficient for every `f`: **`F` (size 25)
  is a reduct.**
- **`S` and `F` are the *only* two reducts -- no third, no partial mix**:
  a set `T` is sufficient iff, for every `(i, e, j)`, `T` intersects
  `{p_i} union flagrun(e,j)`. Fix `i`: if `p_i not in T`, `T` must
  intersect *every* `flagrun(e, j)`, including every length-`1` run
  `{e}` for `e = 1..25` (`j=1` is registered, one of the eight padding
  depths) -- so `T` must contain *every* flag position `e`, i.e.
  `F subset-of T`. So for each `i`, either `p_i in T` or `F subset-of
  T`; the latter is a single global condition (not per-`i`), so either
  `F subset-of T` (covering every `i` at once) or `S subset-of T`
  (covering every `i` individually). **Every sufficient set is a
  superset of `S` or a superset of `F`**, hence every *minimal*
  sufficient set equals `S` or `F` exactly -- no other reduct exists,
  and no set combining part of `S` with part of `F` is minimal (it
  would properly contain whichever of `S`/`F` it is a superset of).
- **`S` is the unique minimum reduct** (the registered planted
  structure): `k in {5, 10, 15} < 25 = |F|` in every family, so `S`
  strictly beats `F` on cardinality in all three. Declared costs below
  make `S` the unique minimum on cost too, not only cardinality.
- **Discernibility family size before superset removal: exactly `200*k`**
  (`1000`, `2000`, `3000`): every `(T_0, T[i,e,j])` pair gives a
  *distinct* difference set `{p_i} union flagrun(e,j)` (distinct `(i,
  e, j)` give distinct sets, by the same argument as tuple-distinctness
  above), and no other cross-verdict pairs exist (every `T[i,e,j]`
  shares verdict `0` with every other `T[i',e',j']`, contributing
  nothing to the family). `200*k` pairs, `200*k` distinct sets.
- **Discernibility family size after superset removal: exactly `25*k`**
  (`125`, `250`, `375`): for `j = 1` (`e = 1..25`), the difference set
  is the singleton-plus-signal pair `{p_i, f_e}` (size 2). For `j >= 2`,
  the difference set `{p_i} union flagrun(e,j)` strictly contains
  `{p_i, f_e}` (the run always includes its own start position `e`) --
  a proper superset of an already-present size-2 set, absorbed by
  `remove_redundant_supersets`. The `25*k` size-2 sets (one per `(i,
  e)`, `j=1`) are pairwise incomparable (two distinct size-2 sets, one
  can only contain the other if equal), so none of *them* is pruned.
  **This is the registered "pruned discernibility family has hundreds
  of sets"**: `125` (Family A), `250` (Family B), `375` (Family C) --
  specific, falsifiable numbers, not a vague "large," registered before
  any code runs.
- **Number of reducts: exactly 2, every family** (`S` and `F` above) --
  registered as a metric result, not assumed to generalize; genuinely
  measured wherever exhaustion completes, and asserted here as a
  claim to be confirmed by the SAT/MaxSAT backends' own behavior where
  it does not (below).

## Why exhaustive cross-check is expected -- but not assumed -- to be infeasible at every registered size

`reduct.exact_reducts()` tests subsets of `candidate_properties` in
increasing size order, pruning supersets of already-found reducts, but
*not* pruning anything before the first reduct is found. Since `S`
(the smaller of the two reducts) has size `k`, every subset of size
`0..k-1` (`sum_{s=0}^{k-1} C(n,s)`) and every subset of size exactly
`k` (`C(n,k)`, all but the reducts individually tested and rejected)
must be tested before pruning has anything to work with -- `C(30,5) =
142,506` for Family A alone, before considering any larger size, each
test an `O(reachable)` partition-based `sufficiency()` call. This is
registered as an *expectation*, not baked into the metric: the
implementation attempts real exhaustion, with a genuine 300-second
wall-clock budget, and records whichever way it actually goes,
per-family, per the standing "the real number is reported honestly
whatever it is" discipline (`time_reproduction.py`'s own module
docstring; `prereg/v5.1-discernibility-scaling.md`'s own precedent of
registering a construction whose feasibility was checked, not assumed).
If exhaustion unexpectedly completes within budget at some family, its
result is used as an additional cross-check on `S`/`F` above (a
strengthening of confidence, not a substitute for the hand-proof, which
holds regardless). If it does not complete, that family's `exhaustive_
cross_check` field is recorded `"infeasible_300s"`, not `null` or
omitted -- an explicit, positive statement of what was tried and how it
ended, `time_reproduction.py`'s own `failed_at`-style honesty applied
here to a deliberately-registered budget rather than an unplanned
failure.

## Declared costs (for the weighted-MaxSAT backend)

Every signal property costs `1.0`; every flag and every padding
property costs `100.0`. `cost(S) = k` (`5`, `10`, `15`); `cost(F) =
2500`. `S` remains the unique minimum-cost sufficient set by the same
"every sufficient set is a superset of `S` or `F`" argument above,
combined with strictly-positive per-property costs (`synthesis.py`'s
own module docstring: a proper sufficient subset always costs strictly
less under positive costs, so the minimum-cost solution is a reduct,
and among the two candidates `S`/`F`, `S` wins by construction, `k <
2500` in every family).

## Metrics (registered, six per backend, three backends, three families)

Three backends (`synthesis.py`, unmodified): `find_any_sufficient_
contract` (SAT), `find_minimum_cardinality_contract` (cardinality
MaxSAT), `find_minimum_cost_contract` (weighted MaxSAT).

- **Discernibility family size before superset removal**:
  `len(build_discernibility_family(...))` -- computed once per family.
- **Discernibility family size after superset removal**:
  `len(remove_redundant_supersets(...))` -- computed once per family.
- **Number of reducts found**: for the exhaustive path, when it
  completes within budget, `len(exact_reducts(...)[0])` directly. For
  the SAT/MaxSAT backends, this repository's synthesis engines return
  *one* contract each, not an enumeration of every reduct -- reported
  per backend as "was the returned contract `S`, `F`, or neither" (a
  three-way categorical outcome), not as a count on that path; the
  numeric "number of reducts" (predicted `2`, every family) is scored
  against the exhaustive path wherever it completes, and stated as the
  hand-proved value above wherever it does not, clearly labelled which
  source each report uses.
- **Exact**: does the backend's returned contract match the
  known-correct answer for that backend's own optimization target --
  `S` for cardinality-MaxSAT (the unique minimum-cardinality reduct)
  and for cost-MaxSAT (the unique minimum-cost reduct); for SAT
  (`find_any_sufficient_contract`), "exact" means *sufficient*
  (verified via `discernibility.verify_contract` against the full
  family, not equality to `S` or `F`) -- the same registered caveat
  `prereg/v5.1-discernibility-scaling.md` already used: this
  registration does not assume the SAT backend returns a minimal
  contract, only a correct (sufficient) one. Cross-checked against
  `reduct.exact_reducts()` directly wherever exhaustion completes
  within the 300-second budget; checked against the hand-derived
  answer above (`S`/`F`) wherever it does not.
- **Wall time**: `time.perf_counter()` around the backend call alone,
  `benchmarks.py`'s/`discernibility_scaling_benchmark.py`'s own
  established pattern. For the exhaustive-cross-check attempt itself,
  wall time up to the 300-second budget (or the real completion time,
  if under budget).
- **Peak memory**: `resource.getrusage(resource.RUSAGE_SELF).ru_maxrss`
  delta around the same call, same established pattern.

## Two-sided: does MaxSAT provide a meaningful advantage over plain SAT?

Registered comparison, decided before any run, scored both ways:

- **Quality**: `cardinality_advantage = len(sat_contract) -
  len(cardinality_maxsat_contract)`. `find_any_sufficient_contract`
  (Glucose3, a satisfiability check, not an optimizer) may return `S`,
  `F`, the trivial all-`n`-properties contract, or anything else
  sufficient -- genuinely unknown in advance, a real SAT-solver
  implementation detail, not assumed. **MaxSAT provides a meaningful
  cardinality advantage on a family iff `cardinality_advantage > 0`**
  (SAT's arbitrary answer is strictly larger than the guaranteed
  minimum) -- registered as a per-family, falsifiable yes/no outcome,
  not assumed to hold uniformly. The **explicitly two-sided outcome
  this document registers in advance, not merely tolerates**: if SAT
  happens to already return `S` (or something equally small) on some or
  all families, `cardinality_advantage = 0` there and MaxSAT is
  reported as providing **no meaningful advantage on that family** --
  a legitimate, reportable result, not a benchmark failure, exactly
  parallel to `prereg/v5.1-discernibility-scaling.md`'s own registered
  refusal to assume SAT returns a minimal contract.
- **Cost of that advantage**: `time_overhead_ratio =
  cardinality_maxsat_wall_time / sat_wall_time` (RC2's iterative
  optimization is expected, but not assumed, to cost more wall-clock
  than a single Glucose3 satisfiability check) -- reported descriptively
  per family, alongside the quality comparison, not folded into a
  single pass/fail verdict: a reader can weigh a real cardinality gain
  against its real time cost directly from the two reported numbers,
  the same "descriptive, not a pass/fail gate" treatment
  `prereg/v5.1-discernibility-scaling.md` already gives wall time and
  memory generally.
- No hypothesis is registered on which way `cardinality_advantage`
  goes at any specific family -- it is measured, not predicted, and
  either outcome (including uniformly zero across all three families)
  is accepted and reported as found.
